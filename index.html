The Hatchery Bottleneck

Larvae can't wait for a better fishing season.

The first weeks of a shellfish's life set the ceiling for everything after. That window depends on long-chain omega-3s — especially DHA — and today hatcheries source them from two places that both break down at the worst possible time.

1

Live algae cultures crash

In-house phytoplankton takes constant labor, space, and lighting — and a single contamination event can wipe out a feeding schedule mid-run.

2

Fish oil is volatile

Fish-oil-based enrichments ride on wild anchovy harvests. El Niño years and cancelled fishing seasons send supply and price swinging.

3

Nutrition varies batch to batch

When DHA levels drift between batches, so do growth and survival — and hatcheries lose the predictability they need to plan seed sales.

The Product

We ferment our own proprietary marine microalga — one that naturally makes DHA its dominant omega-3 — and harvest the whole cells into a liquid concentrate. Nothing is extracted or solvent-refined: the oil stays packaged inside the cell, the way filter feeders find it in nature.

Applications

Starting where omega-3 matters most: the hatchery.

Shellfish hatcheries are our first market — filter-feeding larvae take up whole cells directly, so there's no extraction step between our bioreactor and your tanks.

How It's Made

From a single isolate to a gallon of feed.

Isolate

Our proprietary strain was isolated from the environment, then characterized and bred in partnership with the University of Connecticut.

Ferment

Cells grow in the dark in pre-sterilized, single-use bioreactors, fed a simple carbon source and held at a controlled pH.

Harvest

Whole cells are concentrated — no solvents, no oil extraction — so the DHA stays protected inside the cell membrane.

Feed

The concentrate ships by the gallon, ready to dose into larval tanks, broodstock systems, or live-feed enrichment.

“

Why this microalga? Many omega-3 sources deliver a mix of fats. Our strain makes DHA its dominant long-chain omega-3, with little else competing for space — the fatty acid larvae need most during early development. And because we feed the whole cell rather than an extracted oil, that DHA reaches your larvae still protected inside the cell membrane.

The Bigger Picture

A replacement for fish oil, proven one hatchery at a time.

Most of the world's omega-3 still comes from small wild fish. As aquaculture grows, that supply can't keep up. Fermented microalgae make DHA at the source — the same place fish get it in the first place.

Takes the fish out of the chain

Fish don't make omega-3s — they accumulate them from algae. We skip the middle step and grow the algae directly.

Independent of season and ocean

Fermentation runs year-round, in any climate, close to customers — not tied to a single fishery half a world away.

One platform, many markets

Hatcheries are the beachhead. The same organism and process point toward aquafeed, animal nutrition, and food ingredients.

About AccessFlora

Started in the hatchery. Built in the lab.

I'm Keegan Jalbert. I learned aquaculture hands-on — in Australia, Ecuador, and here in Connecticut — and kept running into the same problem: the farms that feed us depend on a fragile, wild source of omega-3.

So I isolated and bred a DHA-producing microalga and built a fermentation process around it with the University of Connecticut. AccessFlora's mission is simple: give hatcheries a reliable, fish-free omega-3 they can plan around — and grow that platform into a real alternative to fish oil.
